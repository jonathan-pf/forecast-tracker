## Movements

### Will Labour be largest party at next UK General Election?: 38% → 44%
Up from 38% to 44% - the largest single-day move in this tracker, driven by genuinely new information. Andy Burnham became Labour leader on 17 July and Prime Minister on 20 July, and the first wave of post-transition polls shows a pronounced 'Burnham bounce': Deltapoll (17-20 July fieldwork) has Labour up 8 points to 28%, level with Reform, and subsequent polls reported by 24-26 July show Labour overtaking Reform outright, with Burnham's approval ratings already exceeding anything Starmer recorded. Reform is simultaneously squeezed by the Farage £5m Harborne donation story. Yesterday's 38% was anchored on pre-transition averages (Reform ~24.7, Labour ~23.1); with Labour now level-to-ahead on vote share AND retaining superior FPTP seat efficiency (the late-June MRP already made Labour largest party on tied vote share), a meaningful upgrade is warranted. I discount the full bounce because new-leader honeymoons often fade (Brown 2007) - hence 44%, not higher.

### Will UK annual CPI inflation fall below 2.0% for at least one month before 1 January 2027?: 3% → 2%
Down slightly from 3% to 2%. June 2026 CPI (published 22 July) was 2.6%, a 15-month low, helped by falling food (2.2%) and fuel prices - but the forward path has worsened for a YES: the Bank of England's latest projection has CPI at 'a little under 3%' in Q3 2026 and 'a little over 3.25%' in Q4, as Middle East-related energy costs and renewed food price pressure feed through. With only ~4-5 monthly prints (July-November data) publishing before 1 January 2027 and the central expectation now pointing to re-acceleration rather than further decline, a sub-2.0% print would need an implausible ~0.7pp+ downside shock. Analysts also warn autumn energy pressures could drive price spikes. Small downgrade.

### Will Spider-Man: Brand New Day gross over $1.5B worldwide by 31 December 2026?: 88% → 90%
Up from 88% to 90%. The film has not yet opened - release is this Friday, 31 July - but final pre-release signals strengthened again over the weekend: top-end tracking has been revised to $260-300M domestic (vs $230-250M last week), presales are the best first-day of any film in five years and fourth-highest in history across platforms, and China presales are pacing ahead of any comic-book film since Endgame, with the film tracking toward Hollywood's first $100M+ China opening weekend since 2021. Strong China performance was one of the main residual uncertainties, so this materially de-risks the $1.5B path (No Way Home hit ~$1.9B without China). Remaining risk is now mostly legs/reception, which we cannot observe until after opening; modest upgrade with the CI floor raised.
