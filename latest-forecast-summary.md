## Key takeaways

1. **Four central estimates moved, all by a single point.** AGI-by-2030 fell 31 to 30, economic consultancy fell 15 to 14, Labour largest party rose 53 to 54, and UK 2030s GDP growth rose 33 to 34. The other five held. No update trigger fired outright on any of the nine questions.

2. **AGI-by-2030 (30%) slipped on uniform market drift, not on any single event.** Every announcement-resolving leg moved the same way in one session: Kalshi before-2027 9.8 to 7.7, before-2028 19 to 18, before-2030 44.8 to 44.7, Polymarket OpenAI-AGI-2027 about 11 to 10. ARC-AGI-3 stayed frozen at 30.2% for a fourth verified snapshot and no lab made an AGI claim. Partly offsetting, Polymarket now prices public release of OpenAI's next major model at 87% by 31 October, which weakens the prior's headline argument that the plausible announcement vehicles were being indefinitely withheld.

3. **AGI-by-2035 held at 85 with the interval floor returned to 73.** The Goodheart Labs 80% upper bound reverted from 2042 back to 2043 on the 18 August read, undoing the tightening that justified yesterday's one-point lift to the floor. Compute financing strengthened (Nvidia committing up to $105bn of credit for an OpenAI Ohio data centre, SEC staff relief on data-centre debt) against a WSJ finding of roughly $3tn of off-balance-sheet AI commitments across nine tech firms.

4. **The OpenAI/Anthropic ARR question is now close to arithmetically settled.** Bloomberg reported on 17 August that Anthropic's annualised run rate passed $65bn at end-July, up from $47bn in May, carried same-day by CNBC, Axios, Reuters and TechCrunch. Combined with OpenAI's above-$40bn figure that puts qualifying-source ARR near $105bn against a $70bn bar - headroom widened from 23% to 50%. The residual 1% is resolution mechanics (no Q4-dated print yet), not economics.

5. **UK inflation moved further away from the 2% bar.** The July CPI print lands 19 August and is not yet published, but a pre-release consensus near 2.9% has formed on the back of the 13% July Ofgem cap rise. If that lands, the publishable window shrinks from five reference months to four and the required fall widens from 0.65pp to 0.95pp. Energy inputs also hardened: NBP gas back to 154p/therm, Brent at $90.97, the US-Iran ceasefire expired unextended on 17 August.

6. **A methodological correction lifted UK 2030s growth back up.** Chasing the ONS productivity flash (which had not published as of this run) surfaced that ONS recommends its RTI-based productivity measure over the LFS headline the prior forecast leaned on. On the recommended measure output per hour was up 2.1% y/y in Q1 2026, not the 0.4% the prior described as less than half the OBR anchor. Offsetting stress: the 10-year gilt at 5.07-5.09%, Brent at $90.89, and today's labour market release showing vacancies down to 707,000.

7. **Labour edged up on aggregator movement, but seat-level evidence remains contradictory.** PolitPro's aggregate flipped to Labour on 18 August (Lab 24.4, Ref 24.3) and Electoral Calculus's 28 July model puts Labour largest at 58% probability with 244 seats to Reform's 127. But the newest true constituency MRP (More in Common, 19 July) has Reform largest on 183 seats to Labour's 110. The seat-efficiency premium is level-dependent rather than structural, so the interval stays wide at [38, 65].

8. **Taiwan and Spider-Man are both stable.** Taiwan MND reported 5 PLA sorties, 8 PLAN vessels and 7 official ships to 0600 on 18 August, correcting yesterday's over-reading of a single-sortie print; the post-Han Kuang baseline is a low oscillation, not a collapse. Spider-Man: Brand New Day sits at $2,022,832,000 worldwide on The Numbers as of 18 August, $522.8m clear of the bar; Box Office Mojo remains dataless for a fifth consecutive check, now diagnosable as a site-wide ingestion freeze rather than a dispute over this title.
