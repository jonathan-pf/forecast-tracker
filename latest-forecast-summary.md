## Key takeaways

- **AGI timelines moved up on both horizons.** TIME's 26 August cover story *Inside OpenAI's Reboot* put four named OpenAI principals on record: Altman expects an internal system he would call AGI by end-2026, Mark Chen says the company is "80% of the way", Pachocki says Astra already clears an automated-research-intern bar, Brockman says this period may be remembered as when AGI emerged. None of these asserts achievement, so nothing resolved — but the Kalshi KXOAIAGI ladder repriced up across every rung on roughly ten times normal volume, with the before-2028 tranche moving 17 to 37 on a 0.6-cent spread.
- **Restraints on the AGI move are real.** The standing aggregates (Goodheart Labs 2031 median, Metaculus Q5121 January 2033) have now seen this story and stayed flat, MIT Technology Review's 18 August shadow-evaluation study finds agents still cannot do open-ended research, and Polymarket traders give Astra an 80% chance of shipping by 30 September while pricing OpenAI at 3.6% to hold the best model.
- **Labour's structural seat advantage got its best evidence yet.** More in Common's 26 August wave (n=3,140, fieldwork to 23 August) has Labour 27, Reform 23 — Reform's lowest since January 2025 — and crucially it is the first house to prompt for Restore Britain, which took 4% while Reform fell 2. That is close to a controlled test of whether the right-split is real rather than a measurement artefact, and it resolves in favour of real. Survation's MRP for HOPE Not Hate separately sizes Reform's tactical-voting exposure at nearly 300 seats.
- **The consultancy AI-disruption case weakened.** A 9 June National Law Review paper covering the identical industry reaches the opposite conclusion on pace to the ProMarket analysis logged as the strongest up-case evidence — so the requirement for two or more credible analyses converging on AI-caused decline now has two credible analyses pointing in opposite directions. Cornerstone Research's own H1 2026 data show securities filings up 30% on AI-related claims, i.e. AI generating litigation demand for the exact firms in the niche.
- **Four questions held flat**, all for good reasons: Taiwan on an eight-month gap since the last named PLA exercise and an ODNI assessment finding no fixed unification timeline; UK CPI because July printed at 2.9% and only four publishable reference months remain; Spider-Man at $2.23bn against a $1.5bn bar; and the AI ARR question at a combined run rate near $105bn against a $70bn bar.
