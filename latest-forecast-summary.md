## Movements

**AGI before 2030: 28% -> 29%**

Raise from 28% to 29% (CI [15,42] to [16,43], width unchanged). The move rests on one dated, material development. On 7 August OpenAI published 'Responding to the next frontier of critical cyber capabilities' and posted on X that it is 'treating Astra as our first critical model for cybersecurity under our Preparedness Framework' - first time any model has been placed at that tier. Axios (7 Aug, exclusive), TechCrunch, MacRumors and Interesting Engineering all report OpenAI slowing Astra's release, pausing internal activities lacking safeguards, imposing universal monitoring and bringing in government agencies and safety institutes. The Critical threshold means autonomously finding and building working zero-day exploits against many hardened real-world systems, or devising and executing novel end-to-end attack strategies from only a high-level goal. That is long-horizon, adversarial, open-ended agentic competence - not domain-narrow mathematics. It partially resolves the prior's leading uncertainty ('whether the math results prove narrow') toward generality, which is why the net move is up. Trigger status: the UP trigger 'Astra launches publicly with frontier-generality results or is framed as AGI-class' did NOT fire - the opposite occurred, and OpenAI framed the jump as a risk event rather than an AGI milestone. The UP trigger on new AI-originated mathematical results did not fire (only the earlier Gowers/Alon explanatory companion paper). Neither EO 14409 trigger fired: no Federal Register, NIST, CISA or OSTP deliverable by 10 Aug, and no formal abandonment. The DOWN trigger on functioning pre-release review soft-fired - OpenAI voluntarily invited government testing, a de facto substitute for the lapsed framework - which caps the move at +1pp. ARC-AGI-3 (refreshed 7 Aug) still shows Opus 5 at 30.2%, Sol 7.8%, with OpenAI's 38.3% retained-reasoning claim unadjudicated. No lab announced AGI and no leading researcher stated AGI achieved during 6-10 August.

**AGI before 2035: 86% -> 85%**

Down 1pp to 85% (CI [71,93]) - the smallest defensible move, driven by one genuinely new datum. On 7 August Axios reported exclusively (corroborated by TechCrunch 7 Aug, Forbes 9 Aug) that OpenAI has slowed development and release of Astra after concluding it cannot rule out a 'Critical' cyber rating under its Preparedness Framework, the first time any lab has hit that threshold. Astra now faces expanded safety testing, isolated evaluation environments and universal monitoring across agentic deployments, and a White House official confirmed OpenAI 'voluntarily informed the administration of their plans to delay the release.' This cuts both ways. It is a strong capability signal: autonomous zero-day discovery and exploitation is the stated bar. But it is also the first concrete instance of frontier-model deceleration on capability grounds with government in the loop, converting the Pacing the Frontier restraint norm from rhetoric into observed behaviour, and it pushes the prior's downside factor from hypothetical toward real. That justifies a 1pp shave, not more. No formal update trigger fired: the EO 14409 framework remains unpublished, secret and voluntary (TechPolicy.Press; no Federal Register or NIST/CISA deliverables since the 1 Aug lapse), so mandatory pre-release review is not established. Secondary evidence is on-trend or mildly negative. Google's leadership shakeup (Axios and Bloomberg 5-6 Aug: Hassabis to DeepMind Chairman and Alphabet Chief Scientist, Kavukcuoglu taking day-to-day control, Jeff Dean departing) came with reporting that Gemini 3.5 Pro is months behind schedule. Releases 6-10 Aug were incremental (Meta Muse Spark 1.2 on 6 Aug, ByteDance Seedance 2.5 on 8 Aug). BenchLM's 7 Aug ARC-AGI-3 board shows Opus 5 still leading at 30.2% with GPT-5.6 Sol at 7.8%, undercutting the disputed 38.3% figure cited on 5 Aug. Compute capital stayed intact (Sharon AI 6 Aug: ~$8.8B contracted, incl. $4.9B NVIDIA GB300 deal). No lab declared AGI; no researcher declarations.
