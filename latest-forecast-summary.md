## Movements vs previous forecast

### Will economic consultancy lose ≥33% value/jobs by end 2030 due to AI? (20% → 22%, +2pp)

Up 2pp to 22%. The May 4 OpenAI ($4B 'Deployment Company' with TPG +18 investors at $10B pre-money) and Anthropic ($1.5B JV with Blackstone, Goldman, H&F, General Atlantic, Apollo, GIC, Sequoia) consulting ventures explicitly target the $375B management consulting market with a Palantir-style forward-deployed-engineer model. This is the first large-scale, capitalised structural attack on the industry's delivery model — not just AI-augmented incumbents. McKinsey's earlier ~10% non-client cuts and continued 2026 white-collar layoffs reinforce the demand-side erosion narrative.

### Will Labour be largest party at next UK General Election? (3% → 2%, -1pp)

Down 1pp to 2%. May 7 locals are confirming a category-rare collapse: in the first three councils to declare on May 8, Labour lost 26 seats while Reform gained 34. Hartlepool: Reform won 12 of 12. Halton: Reform won 15 of 17. Redditch: Reform won 8 of 9. YouGov 4-5 May voting intention has Reform 25%, Labour 18%, Con 17%, Green 15%, LD 14% — a genuinely fragmented field where Labour is no longer the natural challenger. Electoral Calculus April MRP projects a Reform+Con coalition with Farage as PM. For Labour to be largest party, the electorate would need to shift back ~7-9pp net to Labour from Reform/Greens AND the fragmentation to favour Labour's vote efficiency — neither is consistent with current trajectory.

### Will AGI be developed before 1 January 2035? (75% → 76%, +1pp)

Up 1pp to 76%. The Anthropic-Colossus 1 announcement (220k+ GPUs, May 6) and ongoing 'ASI Finals' compute scaling support the long-horizon view. With ~9 years remaining and frontier-lab compute and capability trajectories continuing to compound, an AGI declaration by end-2034 stays squarely in the dominant outcome space. Resolution criterion (lab declaration OR 3+ researcher endorsement) is materially easier on a 9-year horizon than a 4-year one.

### Will China initiate a military blockade or invasion of Taiwan before 1 January 2030? (9% → 8%, -1pp)

Down 1pp to 8%. Recent expert assessments (AEI April 28, ASPI Strategist) explicitly argue 2026 is not the year; the US government's reported assessment is that Beijing does not plan to attack Taiwan in 2027. PLA activity continues (Luzon Strait task group, March-May airspace restriction), but these read as gradient coercion rather than preparatory. The Iran/Strait of Hormuz precedent is being studied but not adopted. With ~44 months remaining and demonstrable expert consensus that the pre-2027 window is closing without action, the probability of resolving YES drifts down.

### Will UK average annual real GDP growth exceed 1.5% across the 2030s (2030-2039)? (42% → 41%, -1pp)

Down 1pp to 41%. OBR March 2026 EFO continues to anchor 1.1% in 2026 and 1.6% in 2027. IFS expects ~1.4% from 2026 onwards. May 7 local-election results materially confirm the Reform-leaning political trajectory: Reform gaining 30+ seats in first three councils declared, Labour losing 25+. A Reform-led or Reform-Con government would likely produce more aggressive deregulation (planning, AI uptake) but also lower migration, harder EU trade rules, and fiscal volatility — net direction on 2030s GDP is genuinely two-sided. Productivity assumptions and labour-supply outlook remain the cruxes; demographic ageing continues to drag.
