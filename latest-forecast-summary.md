## Key takeaways

**Spider-Man is the big mover (−10pp).** Two new facts drove the cut: IMAX is unavailable for the opening frame (Nolan's *The Odyssey* holds an exclusive IMAX run from 17 July), and the confirmed China release still has no announced date. Doing the multiplier arithmetic explicitly is what forces the correction — a $210-220m domestic opening at a 2.9x multiplier with Spider-Man's typical 57-62% international share lands at ~$1.42-1.60B. The $1.5B bar sits at the *median* of the distribution, not comfortably inside it. Tracking itself is split: Deadline/NRG says $180-190m domestic (implying a clear NO), Boxoffice Pro says $230-250m (a clear YES).

**AGI 2030 up 2pp on a broken assumption.** The prior chain had leaned on ARC-AGI-3 frontier scores being under 1%. That is no longer true: GPT-5.6 Sol now scores 7.8%, a ~20x move in four months on a benchmark built to show we are not close. The increment is deliberately small — 7.8% vs 100% human is still a chasm, and the resolution bar is a public declaration, not a benchmark.

**Labour down 1pp — the prior's reasoning was overstated, not its level.** Restore Britain is polling 3-4%, not ~7%, and Reform is at 24-26%, not 22%. PollCheck's 11 July average still has Reform ~5pts clear (Ref 25.7, Lab 20.3, Con 19.9), so the MRP showing Lab-Ref parity has not been replicated. Electoral Calculus's *own* probability model gives Labour just 26% to be largest party. Offsetting: Burnham's coronation is locked in (conference 17 July) and markets keep Labour a narrow favourite (~34% normalised).

**Taiwan up 1pp on a non-Taiwan input.** US strikes on Iran escalated 11-12 July with the Strait of Hormuz declared closed, and CNN reports US stocks of SM-6, Tomahawk, Patriot and THAAD more than half depleted with 1-4 year rebuild times — on top of the $14bn Taiwan arms package paused since May to conserve munitions. Cross-strait signals themselves stay sub-threshold: continuous CCG presence east of Taiwan is normalisation, not a declared blockade.

**Consultancy held at 24% — genuinely two-sided.** Accenture's 18 June print is worse than the prior captured (bookings −2% YoY, −13% QoQ, guidance cut, stock −20% in a day, with Bloomberg Intelligence blaming AI). But the resolution variables point the other way: BLS professional and business services added 36k jobs in June, the MCA has UK consulting revenue +3% to £21.8bn, and CRA still guides to $785-805m. Equity repricing is not revenue or headcount.

**Four questions held with no change:** AGI 2035 (85%), UK CPI (1%), OpenAI+Anthropic ARR (99%), UK 2030s GDP (37%). In each case new evidence was either absent or near-perfectly offsetting.

## Notable movements this run

- **Spider-Man −10pp** — a second consecutive correction of an over-confident prior (94% → 80% → 70%), now grounded in explicit multiplier arithmetic rather than tracking headlines.
- **AGI 2030 +2pp** — first upward move driven by a benchmark result rather than lab rhetoric.
- **Taiwan +1pp** — US munitions depletion, not cross-strait escalation.
- **Labour −1pp** — level roughly right, underlying reasoning rebuilt on harder polling data.
