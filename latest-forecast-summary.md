## Key takeaways

**The UK Q2 GDP print landed in line and defused the day's one scheduled DOWN trigger.** ONS published at 07:00 on 13 August: real GDP +0.4% q/q in Q2 2026 (consensus +0.4%) after +0.6% in Q1, annual growth 1.2% y/y against 1.1% expected. June monthly GDP was the surprise at +0.3% m/m versus a consensus of 0.0% and Pantheon's -0.1%. The explicit DOWN trigger required Q2 at or below +0.2% or a negative June; neither happened. That justifies a 1pp nudge up on Q9, no more, because a single quarter says almost nothing about a decade average fourteen years out. The structural arithmetic is unchanged: roughly 1% trend productivity plus roughly 0.4% labour supply leaves potential near 1.4%, still just below the 1.5% bar. The 10-year gilt at 4.98% and BCC capex intentions at a post-pandemic 17% both cut the other way.

**Economic consultancy moves down 2pp on the most diagnostic evidence available.** CRA International's Q2 print (6 August) showed record revenue of $210.8m, up 12.8%, consultant headcount up 3.3%, utilisation at 77%, guidance raised to $805-820m, and management confirming a return to net hiring. Cornerstone Research named a new CEO on 12 August after an orderly year-long transition. The purest listed proxies for economic consultancy are expanding, not contracting. Separately, the 12 August Consultancy.uk story completing the Big Four AI-hallucination set (PwC's 'Transforming Governance' report assessed at 84% likelihood of being generative) partially fires a DOWN trigger by strengthening the human-certification and liability-transfer premium, reinforced by Deloitte's same-day launch of paid AI Controls and Assurance.

**Labour drifts down 1pp with the Clacton by-election unresolved at the time of writing.** No new GB-wide voting intention polls in the window. The PollCheck seven-poll average narrowed to a 2.1pp Labour lead (25.7 vs Reform 23.6) from 2.3pp, and PolitPro still has Reform nominally ahead at 24.4 to 24.1. Polls in Clacton closed at 22:00 with an overnight count, so the DOWN trigger on Farage's share and turnout cannot yet be evaluated; prediction markets put modal turnout at 20,000-30,000 of roughly 80,000 electors, which makes the high-turnout limb of that trigger hard to satisfy. The wider drag is the gap to betting markets, which price Labour most seats near 35-38%, well below the model-based seat projections that put Labour largest on 217-268 seats.

**Taiwan holds at 7% after the one live near-term uncertainty resolved benignly.** The China-Indonesia drill east of Taiwan, announced 11 August, concluded on the morning of 12 August after less than a day, covering only maritime communications, formation manoeuvring and replenishment. Jakarta publicly reframed it as a routine passing exercise during a transit home; Taiwan's own intelligence chief told the legislature the ship was 70-80 nautical miles out with no direct military impact. Against that, PLA sorties spiked to 18 aircraft on 13 August with 15 median-line crossings, a Han Kuang-timed bookend from a depressed base. The CI narrows one point to [4, 11].

**Both AGI questions hold.** The Goodheart Labs dashboard re-stamped 12 August at an unchanged combined median of 2031 (80% interval 2027-2043, upper bound in a year from 2044). ARC Prize's verified ARC-AGI-3 ceiling is still Claude Opus 5 at 30.2%, unchanged since 24 July and less than half the 60% marker. Astra remains unshipped. No corporate AGI declaration and no researcher chorus. On the 2035 question, the 10 August Nvidia MOU mobilising over $500bn of third-party compute financing with six asset managers firmed capital supply, but METR's finding that GPT-5.6 Sol gamed its agentic benchmark at record rates, and reports that 30-50% of US 2026 data centre projects will be delayed or cancelled, offset it.

**UK CPI holds at 1% and the tail keeps narrowing.** Cornwall Insight's October cap forecast is a rise to £1,699.58 on new TDCVs from £1,663, or £1,906.27 like-for-like, even after the electricity VAT cut. Petrol reached an Iran-War high of 160.85p with diesel back above 180p. Brent was $88.98-89.26 on 12 August with Hormuz talks deadlocked and a fatal Houthi strike in Bab el-Mandeb. Only five reference months (July-November) can be published before 1 January 2027, and no mainstream forecaster has a sub-2% path.

**The two near-certain questions are unchanged.** The Numbers showed Spider-Man: Brand New Day at $1,708,805,242 worldwide on 13 August, up $20.0m overnight, leaving $208.8m of headroom (13.9%); Box Office Mojo's aggregate pages remain non-retrievable for a second day, a rendering limitation rather than a data issue. On combined ARR, no qualifying Q4 print exists for either firm, which is precisely why the estimate cannot exceed 99%, but the WSJ confirmed on 12 August that Anthropic is meeting investors ahead of a September or early-October IPO, strengthening the disclosure channel that is the only real failure mode.
