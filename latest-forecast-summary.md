## Movements

### Will AGI be developed before 1 January 2030?: -1pp → 26%

Lower to 26% (from 27%). The marginal news since yesterday is restraint-flavoured. On 29 July, OpenAI and Anthropic formally endorsed the Pacing the Frontier plan as companies (not just via individual signatories, now 1,178), explicitly backing mechanisms to slow AI that writes its own code; this institutionalises a norm against racing to, and against triumphally declaring, AGI. The letter also lands ahead of the 1 August deadline under Executive Order 14409's voluntary pre-release review framework, and the Senate Commerce Committee held an AI markup on 29 July - governance machinery is coalescing, though the US government has not yet adopted formal pacing mechanisms (which would trigger a larger downgrade). AGI-26 closes today with its Investor Day; no AGI declaration has emerged, and the conference framing itself stresses that scaling LLMs will not reach full human-level AGI. No new capability results beyond Opus 5's already-priced 30.2% ARC-AGI-3, and OpenAI's post-Sol-incident research slowdown continues. Prediction markets referenced this week put roughly 10% on OpenAI achieving AGI by 2027, consistent with our sub-2030 range. Net: a one-point trim for corporate-level pacing endorsements and no offsetting capability news.

### Will Labour be largest party at next UK General Election?: +1pp → 47%

Raise (+1pp, 46 to 47). Small incremental update on confirming evidence. YouGov 26-27 July (Times/Sky) has Labour tied with Reform at 22% each (Con 21%) - Labour's highest in that tracker since last September, up six points from its 16% May low. More importantly, the PollCheck seven-poll moving average as of 28 July now shows Labour fractionally AHEAD of Reform (24.4% vs 24.3%) for the first time in roughly 16 months, with More in Common (Lab 28, Ref 24) and DeltaPoll (28-28 tie) corroborating the Burnham bounce is holding rather than fading a week-plus after he became PM. On the Reform side, the parliamentary standards probe into Harborne's GBP 5m payment is paused while Farage is not an MP, but the Met Police investigation (Electoral Commission referral) continues. Clacton (13 Aug) is now effectively a coronation - Labour, Tories, Lib Dems and Greens are not standing, leaving Count Binface as lead challenger among a record 34 candidates - so a Farage win is fully priced in and offers limited genuine recovery momentum. No negative developments for Labour in the last 48 hours. This is partial progress toward the 'consistent Labour lead' trigger, warranting a one-point nudge, not a full trigger.

### Will UK annual CPI inflation fall below 2.0% for at least one month before 1 January 2027?: -1pp → 1%

Lower. The late-July oil sell-off has violently reversed: Brent jumped ~6.9% in 24 hours to roughly $89/bbl on renewed Iran-war escalation, with the Strait of Hormuz reported closed for five months and energy costs feeding into UK household bills from July. This kills the main upside path (the 'Brent sustained below $75' trigger is now ~$14 away in the wrong direction). Today's MPC decision (12:00 UK) had not yet been indexed at time of update, but consensus is a 7-2 hold at 3.75% (markets price 96% hold), and pre-release reporting shows the Bank still expects CPI a little under 3% in Q3 and a little over 3.25% in Q4 2026 — a rising path into year-end. Bailey has signalled previously expected cuts are off the table because of the war. With June CPI at 2.6% and only the July-November prints publishable before 1 Jan 2027, sub-2.0% would require a ~0.7pp+ collapse against a projected ~0.7pp rise, in a quarter where oil just spiked. I move the central estimate from 2% to 1% and narrow the interval; will revisit if the MPR (out today) surprises materially dovish on the CPI path.
